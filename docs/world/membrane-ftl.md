# Membrane FTL

This document is the setting contract for faster-than-light travel in *Last
Best Hope*. It defines what FTL can do, what a ship must preserve during
transit, and which failures remain possible. Particular drives, cultures, and
routes may differ without violating these rules.

## Cosmology

The observable universe is a (3+1)-dimensional spacetime membrane embedded in
a higher-dimensional space called the **Dark**. The Dark has stable physical
laws and additional spatial directions.

The speed of light remains the local speed limit. A vessel does not cross its
own membrane faster than light; it leaves the membrane and follows a
higher-dimensional route between locations brought close together by folds.

Time is largely a property of the membrane. The Dark preserves causal order,
so an event cannot precede its causes, but it supplies no universal rate at
which time must pass.

## Transit

A ship makes a transit in five stages:

1. It carries away a microscopic **anchor kernel** of its home membrane.
2. It establishes an isolation field around the vessel.
3. It detaches from the membrane into the Dark.
4. It crosses a shorter higher-dimensional route.
5. It establishes a unique correspondence and reattaches at its destination.

The anchor kernel preserves continuity with the ship's universe. The
isolation field maintains familiar space, time, and physical law aboard. A
ship therefore carries a small region in which membrane reality can continue;
neither system makes the vessel invulnerable.

## Depth and correspondence

**Depth** measures how weakly a vessel corresponds to its membrane. Greater
depth exposes more possible routes and can reveal much shorter ones, but it
does not guarantee a shorter path.

| Depth | Available route | Membrane time relative to ship time | Typical risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Skimming | Modest shortcut | Nearly normal | Low |
| Shallow Dark | Interstellar shortcut | Slow | Moderate |
| Deep Dark | Extreme shortcut | Nearly stopped | Severe |
| Open Dark | No stable correspondence | Undefined | Usually unrecoverable |

Open Dark is not merely another travel band. A vessel there has no reliable
mapping back to the membrane and ordinarily cannot identify a valid place to
reattach.

## Time and duration

The crew experiences ordinary time, $\tau$, inside a healthy isolation field.
For a transit at constant depth, elapsed membrane time is

$$
\Delta t_{\mathrm{membrane}} = C(d)\,\Delta \tau_{\mathrm{ship}},
\qquad C(d) \ge 0.
$$

Normally, $C(d)$ approaches zero as depth increases: weeks aboard may
correspond to seconds on the membrane. For a changing route, the more general
rule is

$$
\Delta t_{\mathrm{membrane}}
= \int C\bigl(d(\tau), \text{local conditions}\bigr)\,d\tau.
$$

Temporal currents can produce $C>1$, so a short voyage aboard consumes years
in normal space. $C$ cannot become negative. No route, current, drive failure,
or reattachment sends a ship into the past.

A ship must actively maintain its own **duration**. If temporal maintenance
fails, processes aboard slow, lose synchronization, and eventually freeze.
This is a loss of carried time, not backward time travel.

## Navigation and reattachment

Every nearby point in the Dark corresponds to a point on the membrane. Folds
overlap at greater depth, making that correspondence increasingly ambiguous.
A navigator can use:

- stellar and gravitational deformations;
- artificial beacons projected into the Dark;
- known exterior routes and currents; and
- the anchor kernel's connection to its home membrane.

Safe reattachment requires all three of the following:

- a unique destination correspondence;
- momentum compatible with the destination region; and
- an intact anchor kernel.

Reattachment preserves what remains. It cannot reconstruct matter,
information, continuity, or duration genuinely lost during transit.

## Transit groups and rendezvous

Ships cannot rendezvous in the Dark. Depth, route, and carried duration do not
provide a shared exterior frame precise enough for two independently detached
vessels to find or approach one another. Detecting another ship's wake does not
establish a navigable correspondence with that ship.

A multi-ship transit group must be established before detachment. Its vessels
coordinate their anchors and isolation fields so the group carries one shared
correspondence through the route. Membership is fixed when the group enters
the Dark, and the surviving members reattach in the same coordinated operation.

If a vessel becomes separated from the group, neither side can turn back,
wait, search, dock, transfer people or material, or conduct a rescue in the
Dark. Each can only attempt reattachment independently and seek the others in
membrane space afterward.

In together, out together, or not at all.

## Coherence

**Coherence** is the vessel's ability to remain one continuous
membrane-physical place. The isolation field prevents higher-dimensional
geometry from acting directly on the ship, but coherence declines with:

- depth and duration of exposure;
- violent maneuvering;
- Dark weather;
- isolation-field damage; and
- contact with Dark organisms.

Coherence failure progresses through observable stages:

1. sensor and timing discrepancies;
2. spatial distortion;
3. desynchronization between compartments;
4. intrusion from the exterior; and
5. loss of the vessel as a single continuous object.

The sequence allows warning, damage control, retreat, and lasting consequences
before terminal loss. A severe or neglected failure may still skip stages.

## The Dark

The Dark is a lawful ecology, not chaos. Its organisms are stable under
higher-dimensional physics; matter native to a membrane is not. Known or
suspected organisms may:

- feed on curvature or temporal fields;
- follow a vessel's coherence wake;
- use isolation fields as habitable pockets;
- intersect sealed interiors through additional dimensions;
- migrate along stellar folds; or
- remain dormant until exposed to a ship's carried time.

Ships leave temporary wakes of geometry and ordered duration. Repeated wakes
make established routes easier to follow, but may also attract organisms that
feed, travel, or hunt along them.

The physical, ecological, and encounter rules for this life are defined in
[Dark ecology](dark-ecology.md). The normal-space stellar, orbital, radiation,
and habitability facts navigators use to choose a destination are defined in
the [stellar-system model](stellar-system-model.md).

## Infrastructure and communication

Interstellar civilization depends on:

- **lift zones** in regions of low gravitational gradient;
- **landing fields** that stabilize reattachment;
- **Dark beacons** that mark exterior geography;
- **anchor vaults** that authenticate vessels and origins; and
- **route observatories** that track folds and temporal currents.

There is no universal FTL radio. Interstellar communication uses couriers,
low-bandwidth Dark beacons, or deliberately stabilized links. Information is
therefore constrained by routes, infrastructure, and the risks borne by the
ships carrying it.

## Invariants

All technology, events, and mechanics involving membrane travel must preserve
these constraints:

1. Nothing moves locally faster than light.
2. Nothing travels backward in Dark causal order.
3. Greater depth offers more extreme shortcuts but weaker correspondence.
4. A ship must carry and maintain its own stable space and duration.
5. An anchor preserves continuity, not invulnerability.
6. Reattachment cannot restore what was genuinely lost.
7. Independently detached ships cannot meet in the Dark; a transit group's
   membership is fixed at entry.
8. The Dark obeys consistent laws even when people cannot perceive or model
   them completely.

The deeper a ship travels, the less distance and outside time matter—and the
harder it must work to remain a place where human reality can continue.
