# Dark ecology

This document defines the ecological contract for native life in the Dark. It
extends the cosmology in [`membrane-ftl.md`](membrane-ftl.md) and guides creature
generation, Passage encounters, route simulation, reports, and visual design.

Dark organisms are not supernatural and the Dark is not chaos. Its life follows
stable higher-dimensional physics. Fleet instruments reveal partial,
condition-dependent evidence of organisms whose complete bodies cannot fit
inside membrane space.

## Physical model

A Dark organism has three familiar spatial axes and at least one additional
spatial axis, represented by `w`. A membrane observer sees only the intersection
between that body and the local membrane correspondence. This visible
three-dimensional intersection is a **manifestation**.

Time is not another geometric axis in the creature model. It governs movement,
metabolism, growth, injury, reproduction, and ecological succession. A living
organism changes continuously with time while local physical conditions select
which part of its higher-dimensional anatomy can manifest.

The observed body can be expressed as:

$$
M(x,y,z,\tau) = D\bigl(x,y,z,w(E, A, d),\tau\bigr),
$$

where:

- $D$ is the organism's higher-dimensional body field;
- $E$ is the local environment;
- $A$ is the observer's anchor and isolation-field state;
- $d$ is Dark depth;
- $w$ is the physical slice selected by those conditions; and
- $\tau$ is locally carried duration.

An organism does not transform arbitrarily when `w` changes. Organs enter,
connect, divide, and leave the manifestation because the membrane intersects a
different continuous region of the same body.

## Conditions that select a manifestation

No single instrument should report a universal `w` coordinate. Operationally,
the visible slice depends on measurable local conditions:

- curvature and gravitational gradient;
- correspondence width and ambiguity;
- isolation-field strength and modulation;
- anchor-kernel resonance;
- temporal-current intensity;
- radiation and electromagnetic fields;
- the geometry and turbulence of recent wakes; and
- nearby concentrations of membrane matter.

Each species has different sensitivities. Two organisms in the same route may
therefore manifest at different field settings. Deliberately changing an
isolation field can reveal or repel anatomy, but it also changes the ship's
coherence protection.

The simulation should derive manifestation from stable species traits and
current conditions. Presentation time may animate a body but must not replace
those causes with unseeded visual noise.

## Energy and material cycles

Dark ecosystems are organized around gradients. Native organisms exploit
differences that membrane matter cannot ordinarily reach across:

- **Curvature gradients** supply energy around folds, massive bodies, and
  changing correspondences.
- **Temporal gradients** arise where carried duration meets regions with weak or
  irregular time.
- **Coherence gradients** surround isolation fields and newly detached membrane
  matter.
- **Wake gradients** retain disturbed geometry, ordered duration, heat, and
  trace material after transit.
- **Radiative gradients** cross different higher-dimensional paths than matter
  and can accumulate in sheltered structures.

Matter still matters. Organisms may incorporate dust, hull material, atmospheric
compounds, or biological molecules into temporary membrane-facing organs. Most
native biomass and energy storage remain outside the visible slice.

The basic food web is:

1. Primary organisms harvest stable curvature, radiation, or temporal
   gradients.
2. Grazers consume their fields, tissues, or stored gradients.
3. Predators exploit grazers and other mobile organisms.
4. Scavengers and decomposers reclaim disrupted bodies and exhausted wakes.
5. Habitat-forming colonies alter routes and shelter later communities.

Ships add a new resource pulse. Their wakes can increase primary production,
carry propagules, feed specialists, and concentrate predators. A well-traveled
route can become easier to navigate and more biologically occupied at the same
time.

## Ecological roles

These are roles, not fixed taxonomic groups. One species may occupy different
roles during different life stages.

### Gradient producers

Sessile or slowly migrating organisms establish themselves around folds,
temporal currents, and stable correspondence boundaries. They convert
environmental gradients into structured tissue. Their manifestations often
resemble laminae, nested cavities, branching filters, or discontinuous fields of
small apertures.

They are the base of many Dark food webs. Damage to a visible patch may affect a
much larger connected body.

### Wake feeders

Wake feeders follow or colonize the disturbed geometry left by transit. Some
remove turbulence and make an old route more legible. Others deepen ruts,
increase ambiguity, or consume the ordered duration that navigation relies on.

They do not chase ships through a shared exterior frame. They respond locally
to a wake after passage, consistent with the prohibition on independent Dark
rendezvous.

### Isolation colonists

These organisms exploit the boundary of a vessel's carried membrane pocket.
They may feed on leakage, attach propagules, or use the field as a temporary
habitat. Their manifestations can cross sealed compartments because their full
bodies remain connected outside the ship's three-dimensional interior.

Colonization should progress through observable stages: field distortion,
repeatable sensor intersections, material exchange, established growth, and
only then serious coherence intrusion. Crews must have opportunities to study,
contain, tolerate, redirect, or remove it.

### Fold grazers

Large mobile organisms sweep feeding structures across productive folds. A
single passage may reveal a sequence of apparently separate bodies: filters,
storage cavities, locomotor tissue, and trailing symbionts. Repeated observation
can establish that these were connected manifestations.

Their migrations alter route conditions and provide moving habitats for smaller
organisms.

### Shear predators

Predators approach prey through additional spatial directions. They need not
cross the visible distance between two manifestations. Their attacks are first
detected through displaced tissue, interrupted signals, defensive reactions, or
localized coherence pressure.

Predation still has constraints. A predator must detect prey, maintain a useful
correspondence, overcome its defenses, and preserve its own body. It cannot
appear wherever a story needs a threat.

### Reef bodies

Long-lived colonies connect several distant manifestations through the Dark.
What appears to be growth on separate surfaces—or at separate membrane
locations—may belong to one organism. Reefs shelter nurseries, scavengers,
parasites, and symbionts and may retain a history of route traffic in their
structure.

Reefs make places ecologically persistent. Removal, harvesting, or protection
should change later route conditions and encounters.

### Duration scavengers

These organisms become active around failed temporal maintenance, abandoned
isolation pockets, and old wakes. They consume stored chemical order, remaining
field structure, or slow biological processes. They are associated with stalled
machinery and desynchronized material, not backward time travel.

Some may clean dangerous remnants. Others may continue feeding on a damaged
ship's carried duration.

## Adaptation

Dark anatomy evolves under pressures that do not exist in membrane ecology.

- **W breadth:** broad bodies tolerate changing conditions but intersect more
  hazards and are easier to detect.
- **W specialization:** narrow bodies exploit particular gradients efficiently
  but disappear when conditions shift.
- **Dimensional segmentation:** injury to one region does not propagate through
  the entire body.
- **Slice redundancy:** different intersections can perform the same biological
  function.
- **False manifestations:** expendable anatomy resembles feeding, sensory, or
  reproductive tissue.
- **Environmental tracking:** the body moves along `w` to remain coupled to a
  useful physical regime.
- **Phase camouflage:** visible tissue resembles local debris, infrastructure,
  or another organism at the current slice.
- **Distributed sensing:** structures separated in a manifestation remain
  connected through higher-dimensional tissue.

These adaptations require tradeoffs. No species should possess unrestricted
movement, perfect concealment, complete resilience, and effortless access to
membrane interiors.

## Movement and behavior through time

Time-dependent change should remain distinguishable from environmental slicing.

Biological motion may:

- shift and pulse organs;
- rotate tissue through planes involving `w`;
- propagate contractions through a connected body;
- open feeding cavities when a gradient rises;
- move sensory structures toward a wake;
- withdraw vulnerable anatomy from coherence pressure; or
- expose reproductive tissue during a migration.

Changing `w` reveals different anatomy. Advancing time shows that anatomy doing
something. Both may occur together, but reports and mechanics should preserve
the distinction.

## Life cycles and succession

Birth may begin outside the fleet's observable slice. Propagules first appear as
small disconnected manifestations, then join as growth expands through `w`.
Reproduction may use budding, spore-like anchor particles, exchange between
overlapping bodies, or a mobile life stage carried by wakes.

Death does not make the whole body vanish at once. Manifestations can disappear
as support fails elsewhere, and remains can reappear when conditions select a
different slice. Scavengers, reef builders, and dormant propagules create a
succession sequence after a large organism dies.

Seasonal and route-scale changes should therefore produce ecological history:

- new traffic creates a wake bloom;
- grazers follow increased primary production;
- predators concentrate after the grazers;
- abandoned traffic starves specialists;
- reefs stabilize or obstruct the old route; and
- later expeditions encounter material evidence of the sequence.

## Perception and evidence

The fleet cannot initially observe complete organisms. Encounters should begin
with concrete evidence:

- repeatable intersections at particular field settings;
- coordinated motion among separated visible structures;
- a changed wake or correspondence profile;
- material transferred across a sealed boundary;
- tissue that returns when earlier conditions are reproduced;
- feeding, avoidance, pursuit, or defensive responses; and
- persistent scars on organisms, ships, reefs, or routes.

Classification remains provisional. A report may record that two
manifestations moved together under field modulation. It should not declare
them one mind, a parent and offspring, or hostile without evidence.

Sensors should disagree in bounded, useful ways. Gravitational instruments may
estimate higher-dimensional extent; field telemetry may identify sensitivity;
chemical sampling may characterize only the manifested tissue. Repeated
observations improve confidence rather than removing all uncertainty at once.

## Cognition and social organization

Higher-dimensional continuity complicates the membrane concepts of body and
individual. Several manifestations may be organs of one organism. One visible
body may be a temporary overlap among several organisms. Neither possibility
should be treated as the answer in every encounter.

If intelligent Dark life exists, it may identify individuals through persistent
higher-dimensional continuity rather than visible form. Communication could use
controlled field changes, exchanged tissue, route modification, or patterned
manifestation. Memory may be stored in long-lived anatomy and altered by injury.

The fleet must establish agency through repeated response and retained history.
Unexplained complexity alone is not proof of intelligence.

## Relationship to ships

A ship is not only an intruder. It is a moving habitat, a source of gradients,
and a carrier between ecological regions. Contact can produce:

- hull or field colonization;
- mutual exchange of heat, material, shelter, or navigation;
- accidental transport of propagules;
- predation or defensive injury;
- route cleaning or route obstruction;
- dependence on regular fleet traffic; and
- changed behavior after repeated encounters with a named ship.

Dark contact should create persistent ship history. A vessel may retain a field
scar, altered isolation procedure, resident symbiont, disputed contamination,
specialized sensor practice, or obligation to revisit an affected route.

Recoverable setbacks are preferred. Organisms can drain resources, damage
coherence, alter a route, contaminate cargo, separate compartments in duration,
or force reattachment without making every contact an abrupt run-ending attack.

## Simulation model

The current `Outer_Dark` model already tracks species, weather, wakes, routes,
and infrastructure. Ecology should extend those records without making
presentation state authoritative.

Each species needs deterministic traits for:

- ecological role and preferred depth;
- environmental sensitivities that select its manifestation;
- wake attraction and field attraction or avoidance;
- energy reserve, growth, reproduction, and dormancy;
- mobility across routes or correspondence regions;
- effects on wake turbulence and navigability;
- interactions with other ecological roles; and
- known observations and classification confidence.

Each local population needs persistent state for abundance, life stage,
condition, recent feeding, colonization, migration, and attributable encounters.
Population changes should consume deterministic campaign draws and remain
reproducible for a supplied seed.

Ecological updates should occur at campaign-scale intervals rather than every
rendered frame. A useful order is:

1. Update Dark weather and available gradients.
2. Deposit and decay route wakes.
3. Calculate primary production and feeding.
4. Resolve migration, predation, and colonization.
5. Resolve reproduction, dormancy, injury, and death.
6. Persist route effects and surface observable changes.

Creature rendering receives a genome, environmental slice, biological phase,
and current injuries from simulation state. It must not invent traits or change
the population.

## Encounter design

A Dark ecology encounter should define:

1. **Evidence:** what the crew can directly observe.
2. **Physical cause:** which gradient, wake, field, or organism produced it.
3. **Ecological role:** how the organism obtains energy and affects other life.
4. **Current behavior:** what it is doing over time.
5. **Sensitivity:** which controllable condition changes its manifestation or
   behavior.
6. **Stakes:** what is at risk and which uncertainty remains.
7. **Persistent result:** what changes on a ship, route, population, chart, or
   institution.

Good choices operate on physical relationships:

- alter isolation-field modulation;
- reduce thrust and stop feeding a wake;
- expose a controlled source of heat or duration;
- change depth;
- collect a manifested sample;
- tolerate colonization under quarantine;
- divert traffic from a nursery route;
- maintain a route on which an organism now depends; or
- reattach before coherence falls further.

Avoid choices that reduce contact to “attack, study, or leave” when the ecology
supports a more specific intervention.

## Initial ecology

The first implemented food web should remain small enough for the player to
learn through recurrence.

### Wake film

A distributed primary organism grows in recent transit wakes. It consumes
geometric turbulence and carried heat. Young film slightly reduces turbulence;
dense old film narrows correspondence and attracts grazers.

### Lantern grazer

A broad, migratory organism feeds on wake film. Different field settings reveal
filter tissue, storage chambers, and locomotor lobes. Its migration can clear an
overgrown route while temporarily increasing ambiguity.

### Hush colony

A small symbiont grows within the grazer's radiative structures and suppresses
their signal. It can also colonize ship isolation boundaries, where it reduces
some sensor emissions while consuming field capacity.

### Shear hunter

A narrow predator detects exposed grazers and active isolation fields. It
attacks across `w`, leaving localized field scars and displaced manifestations.
It avoids regions with unstable correspondence because its specialized body
cannot tolerate broad slice changes.

### Grave reef

The remains of old gradient producers form persistent habitat around abandoned
folds. Wake film, dormant grazers, scavengers, and transported propagules occupy
its cavities. Fleet traffic can reopen the reef as a route, damage it, or carry
part of its community elsewhere.

This web supports observation, predation, symbiosis, colonization, succession,
and route engineering without requiring intelligent contact.

## Visual language

Dark organisms follow the project's engraving direction. Their manifestations
use shells, laminae, capillaries, nested voids, cellular fields, and repeated
cavities. Avoid default tentacles, wet gore, faces, or decorative anatomy.

A manifestation should change coherently rather than boil with uniform motion.
Keep most of the image still. Let a feeding aperture cross the slice, a lobe join
another structure, or a field of pores close in sequence. Use a small ship,
beacon, or known structure to establish scale.

Disconnected visible pieces should share construction logic, motion, surface
marks, or response timing so later observations can support a relationship
between them without explaining it immediately.

## Naming

Fleet names are provisional and observational. Prefer names based on measurable
behavior or structure: wake film, shear hunter, lantern grazer, grave reef,
isolation colonist. Different ships or communities may preserve competing names
until a shared classification is enacted.

Avoid names that certify motive, intelligence, malice, sanctity, or a complete
anatomical interpretation.

## Invariants

All Dark ecology systems and encounters must preserve these constraints:

1. Dark life obeys stable physical and ecological rules.
2. `w` is an additional spatial direction; time drives change but is not a fifth
   geometric axis.
3. A manifestation is a condition-dependent intersection, not the whole body.
4. Environmental and field changes cause reproducible changes in the visible
   slice.
5. Organisms require energy, material, habitat, and viable life cycles.
6. Additional dimensions permit unfamiliar access, not unrestricted movement or
   omnipotence.
7. Independent vessels and organisms cannot use Dark contact to violate transit
   rendezvous or causal-order rules.
8. Encounters begin with observable evidence and preserve uncertainty where the
   fleet lacks evidence.
9. Ecological contact can persist in ships, routes, populations, institutions,
   and later choices.
10. Simulation outcomes remain deterministic for a supplied seed.
